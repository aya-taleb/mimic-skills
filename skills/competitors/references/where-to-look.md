# Where to look

Search the way a buyer would. The buyer's first page of results is the real shortlist.
Read pages one at a time, like a person. No scraping, no logins, no bulk downloads.

## Local businesses

1. **Map or places search.** If a places or maps tool is connected, search the category near
   the address ("lunch delivery near South Lake Union, Seattle", "nail salon near 98040").
   Note name, distance, hours and the listing link. If no tool is connected, run a web search
   for the same phrase and open the map listing pages it returns.
2. **The rival's own site.** Menu, price list, booking page, packages. This is the best
   source for a price.
3. **Delivery and booking apps.** Public menu pages on delivery apps, booking pages for
   salons and clinics. Prices there are often higher than in store; say which you saw.
4. **Local community pages.** Neighborhood forums, city subreddits, local news "best of"
   lists. Good for substitutes and for complaints in buyers' own words.
5. **Substitutes people really use.** The office cafeteria, a food truck pod, meal prep at
   home, a friend who cuts hair, a big-box store. Search "<problem> <city> reddit" to see
   what locals do.

What to record for local: distance from the founder's spot, hours, price points from menus or
posted lists, what is included (portion, delivery fee, tip), review rating and count only if
shown on a public page you link.

## Online businesses

1. **Problem search in buyer words.** "how do I <job>", "best tool for <job>", "<job>
   software for <buyer>".
2. **"Alternative to <rival>" searches.** These pages list the rivals buyers compare.
3. **App stores and directories.** App store listings, software directories, browser
   extension stores, marketplace listings. Use public pages only.
4. **Launch sites.** Product Hunt and similar, for new entrants.
5. **Forums.** Reddit, Hacker News, niche communities, Indie Hackers. Threads asking "what
   do you use for X" show substitutes and complaints at once.
6. **Pricing pages.** Free tier, entry tier, what the paid tier adds, contract length,
   per-seat or per-use. Check the page directly; third-party price lists go stale.

## Complaints

- Public review pages: map listings, app store reviews, software directories, Trustpilot.
- Forum threads and comments.
- The rival's own help center or status page (repeated issues show up there).
- Read enough to see patterns, usually 20 to 40 reviews per main rival. Stop when new pages
  repeat what you have.
- Prefer recent reviews (last 12 months). Say if a pattern is old.

## When the user has data

If the user exports their own reviews, a survey, an app store CSV they are allowed to use, or
their sales notes, use that first and say it is their data. It beats anything public.

## When nothing turns up

Say what you searched, where, and that it came back empty. Then widen the scope one step
(the next town, the adjacent category) and say you did. Do not fill the table with guesses.
