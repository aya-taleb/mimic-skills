# Cheap demand tests

Pick the test that puts the riskiest assumption on the line for the least money and
time. Each one asks people to do something, because what people do is the evidence.

## Fake door

A button, menu item or ad for a thing that doesn't exist yet. Count clicks, then show an
honest "not ready yet, want to hear when it is?" page.

- Tests: does anyone want this enough to click?
- Cost: an afternoon, plus any ad spend.
- Honest version: say on the next screen that it isn't built. Never take payment for it.
- Weakness: clicks are cheap. Treat it as a first filter.

## Waitlist

A one-page site with the promise and an email signup.

- Tests: will people trade an email for it?
- Cost: an afternoon, plus traffic (ads, posts, the founder's network).
- Measure: signups divided by unique visitors, from the page's own analytics.
- Weakness: emails are cheap too. Add one question to the form ("what would you use it
  for?") to sort real interest from curiosity.

## Pre-order

A page that takes a real payment or deposit, for a clear delivery date, with a clear
refund promise.

- Tests: will people pay before it exists? This is the strongest cheap signal.
- Cost: a payment link and a page.
- Honest version: state the delivery date and that they can get a full refund. Check
  consumer law where they sell. Refund on request, no friction.
- Weakness: needs a clear offer and a believable date.

## Concierge

Do the service by hand for a few real customers before building anything. A meal plan
written by hand, a lunch route run with a rented van, reports made in a spreadsheet.

- Tests: does the outcome solve the problem well enough that they pay again?
- Cost: the founder's time.
- Measure: how many pay for round two.
- Weakness: doesn't test scale, and the founder's personal touch can flatter the result.

## Setting the lines

Write the pass and fail lines before the test starts, and lock them with the tool. If
you set them after, you'll set them to whatever happened.

- Base the pass line on what the business needs, not on what sounds normal. Work back
  from the goal: "To reach 50 paying teams by June from a 3% trial-to-paid rate, I need
  this page to turn at least X% of visitors into trials."
- If you don't have the founder's numbers, label the line as a starting assumption and
  say why you picked it.
- Set a minimum sample. A 10% signup rate from 20 visitors is two people.
- Set an end date. Tests that never end never fail.
- Between the fail line and the pass line is "unclear". That's a real result: change one
  thing and run it again, or stop spending on it.
